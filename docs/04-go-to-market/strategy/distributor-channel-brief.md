# Distributor Channel Brief
### US Foods & Chef's Warehouse — Underexplored, Highest-Leverage

> **Sim signal:** US Foods at round 12 — *"Distributor reps are already getting asked about CarabinerOS. If it streamlines ordering without creating vendor lock-in or messing up our commission tracking, I'll push it to my accounts."* This was the most underexplored angle in Sim 2. No other agent engaged with it. The operators were debating AI philosophy while the reps were already asking the right question.

---

## Why Distributors Are the Right Channel

**The operator trust barrier drops to zero when a rep recommends it.**

Operators buy from their rep. They trust their rep. If a US Foods rep says "this tool makes my life easier and your ordering cleaner," the operator doesn't need to evaluate CarabinerOS — the rep already did.

This bypasses:
- The "Screen 16" narrative (reps don't care about UI philosophy)
- The implementation survival concern (reps handle onboarding friction)
- The AI skepticism cycle (reps are results-focused, not tech-focused)

---

## What Reps Actually Care About

**Commission flow, not AI.** Reps earn on volume. If CarabinerOS makes ordering more consistent and reduces "I forgot to order" situations, they sell more product. That's the entire pitch.

The rep's pitch to operators: "This tool helps you never run out, and helps me plan my truck better."

**What to NOT say to a rep:**
- "AI-powered procurement"
- "Natural language interface"
- "Restaurant software revolution"

**What to say to a rep:**
- "Your accounts will order more consistently"
- "You'll get earlier lead time on large orders"
- "Your commission doesn't change — the operator's process just gets cleaner"

---

## US Foods — Entry Point

**Profile:** Largest US foodservice distributor. NYC accounts are managed by regional reps out of their Secaucus, NJ distribution center. Territory reps typically manage 40–80 restaurant accounts.

**The ask:** Not a partnership. Not a co-marketing agreement. A pilot with 2–3 NYC-area reps who agree to mention CarabinerOS to their independent restaurant accounts and report back on operator interest.

**Value exchange:** CarabinerOS gets warm introductions. Reps get a tool that makes their accounts more reliable orderers. No money changes hands.

**Risk to US Foods:** Zero. They're not endorsing software — they're answering a question their accounts are already asking.

**Contact strategy:** Go through the restaurant accounts first. If a beta operator uses US Foods, ask them to introduce CarabinerOS to their rep. Never cold-approach a corporate distributor channel without an operator referral.

---

## Chef's Warehouse — Entry Point

**Profile:** Premium specialty distributor. NYC is their home market. Accounts skew toward chef-driven independent restaurants — exactly the CarabinerOS target archetype.

**Difference from US Foods:** Chef's Warehouse reps have deeper relationships with chefs (not just GMs). They're more likely to know the chef-owner personally, which is where CarabinerOS decisions actually get made.

**The ask:** Same pilot structure as US Foods, but framed around specialty ordering accuracy. Seasonal menus + specialty items = the highest ordering error rate. That's the pain point.

**Personalization hook:** Chef's Warehouse already has a digital ordering portal (ChefStoreApp). CarabinerOS doesn't compete with it — it generates the orders that flow into it. That's the integration story: "We draft the order. You send it through your existing portal."

---

## Pilot Structure (Proposal)

**Phase 1 — Operator-led introductions (Month 1–2):**
Get 3–5 NYC beta operators on CarabinerOS. Ask each one to introduce CarabinerOS to their primary US Foods or CW rep. Gather rep feedback.

**Phase 2 — Rep pilot (Month 3):**
2 reps (1 US Foods, 1 Chef's Warehouse) agree to mention CarabinerOS to 5 of their accounts each. Measure: how many take a demo? How many convert?

**Phase 3 — Rep-initiated outreach (Month 6+):**
If pilot shows rep-referred accounts convert faster, expand to 10 reps. Build a "rep referral" page that lets reps send a branded intro link to their accounts.

---

## The Anti-Lock-In Framing (Critical)

Chef's Warehouse rep at Sim 2 was cautious about "vendor lock-in." This must be addressed pre-emptively:

- CarabinerOS works with any distributor. It doesn't lock operators to any supplier.
- Reps don't lose accounts if operators use CarabinerOS — they keep them (and they order more consistently).
- Operator data (order history, par levels) is exportable. Not held hostage.

**The line for reps:** "We make your accounts better customers. We don't change who they buy from."
